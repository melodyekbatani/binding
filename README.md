## Overview
Binding is a collection of readings design and developed using HTML and CSS and my first complete, responsive full website project. This project brings together three key readings that frame typography as an organizing principle rather than an expressive stylistic layer to design. This site explores typography as a guiding principle.

## Context
This was my third project for Type and Interaction! This collection of readings is based on 3 key readings around the theme of typography as an organising principle within design. Including Anthony Froshaugh’s Typography is a Grid, Lorraine Wild’s Castles made of Sand and Silvia Sfligiotti’s This is Autotune Type. In understanding typography as a methodology for decision-making, I've begun to realise how design systems can clarify ideas, reveal their limitations, or quietly flatten them into a singular "default” variety. This collection brings together three distinct viewpoints on the role of typography and the larger design context in which it is situated. The project traces typography across moments of structured principle, instability, and automation. Through which, it further examines how the systems we build organize meaning and redistribute authorship between designer and tool. The distillation of this is reflected in the exploration of type as a guiding principle, realised through the web's grain.

I also designed a series of 2 images - one GIF and one SVG that reflect the relationship between the three texts. Within the GIF, I explore the idea of finding a grid, extracting and manipulating it into various contextual points, and how when mass applied, it becomes a mog-pog of similar stylistic templates. I show a shell being extracted, broken down by hands moving and manipulating it, and the amalgamation of the points into a version of typography that starts to look and feel the same.

Within the svg, I wanted to show these readings in relation to one another, a series of blocks that start as a strict linear pattern that start to shift and break down. Finally, the blocks follow the form’s found in music, showing a cascading effect of auto-tune typography as becoming one and the same throughout every context.

## Systems
Throughout this process, I introduced systems for both creating responsive design and customized it throughout each readings. I began by analyzing the reading and picking key elements to integrate in the design. This process included pulling key quotes and developing a system for sub-headers to make the design easier to follow. I implemented this into the semantic structure, breaking down each reading as distinct parts of a whole. Once I established a strong hierarchy, I began experimenting with typography and applied various sizing and vertical spacing to break from the web's default design while optimizing it for readability across various breakpoints. I began styling through mobile-first design and found ways to optimize the design throughout various breakpoints.

## Results
Responsiveness was central to the project’s logic. I began by designing the mobile-first experience and extended the system outwards to desktop and middle breakpoints. I introduced the idea of friction through spacing, shifts in the scaling and alignment changes. One key element that was prevalent in the text was these long footnotes, which I knew I wanted to integrate into the design. I decided to integrate these through the use of a small monospace font that made the user feel like these additional footnotes were optional to read, but were accessible if the user was interested.

## Results
Building this project was a foundational step in understanding how typography and systems shaped code through the web’s grain. Working on this project shaped my ability to think structurally, work with constraints and shape a conceptual framework through design. My process is a direct reflection of the three readings, showing me that typography is a framework for expression, and that setting up rules can lead to interesting outcomes!
